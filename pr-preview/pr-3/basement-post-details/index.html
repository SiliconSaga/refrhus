Basement post geometry

Plan-view details for the six basement posts, drawn in isolation because their relationships to the joists and girders are too fine to read in the Sweet Home 3D plan.

Source lives in the house repo at hoards/refrhus/basement-post-details.html; the published page is generated from it. Every dimension is either measured in the basement or derived under a stated assumption. The datum is the south interior cinder face at model y = 511.0 — the one line the basement shares with a Main-floor exterior wall.

How the paneling thickness fell out

Three west-zone tape runs that overlap rather than abut — and that overlap is what makes them solvable.

Two chains that meet

The bathroom chain validates itself. Round two walked from the north paneling through the door frame and bar to the bathroom's inner south wall, landing at 76.125″. Round three walked from bare cinder inside the drain-stack void to the same wall, landing at 77.875″. The 1.75″ difference is the paneling, and it means twelve hand measurements across two sessions agree to the sixteenth.

North and south chains meet within a quarter inch. Working south from the cinder through the den puts the NW post's north face 86.0625″ off the north block. The girder relationship — 0.375″ proud at NW, ≈2.5″ to each post's south face, 3.125″ thick — then predicts the N post's north face at 85.125″. Round three's independent chain, running the other direction entirely, measures 84.875″. Nothing in that comparison assumes the shell is rectangular, so the two halves of the basement genuinely register against each other.

Residual uncertainty

The south paneling is the one assumed number. No bare block is exposed on the den's south wall, so 1.1875″ is what remains after the utility zone's shell rather than a measurement. Assuming it instead matches the north's measured 1.75″ implies a west-zone shell of 272.8125″ against the utility zone's 272.25″ — 0.56″ apart, below the ±1″ of joist drift already carried on the NW and N posts.

  • Joist drift. The grid was read 5–7 ft off the south wall. SW sits near that band; NW and N are 15 ft away, where the recorded ½″-per-few-feet drift could move their east–west reference by up to an inch.
  • The bar counter does not fit the tape chain. The counter was seen overhanging the bathroom's west wall by about 3″, but the placement here makes the wall the more westerly. Likeliest reconciliation is a countertop overhanging its own base. Nothing structural depends on it.
